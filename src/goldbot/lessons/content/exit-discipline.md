title: Exit Discipline
---

## What it is

A stop answers one question: *was I wrong?* An exit rule answers a different
one: *is the reason I entered still true?*

Here the exit is a close below the 20-day average — the same window that
generated the entry. The trade was taken because buyers had control over that
horizon. When price closes below the average of that horizon, they no longer
do, and the premise has expired whether or not the trade made money.

Most beginners have a stop and no exit rule. The result is a system where every
winning trade is held until it stops winning, which converts a decent strategy
into a poor one with remarkable consistency.

## When it works

It is what turns the asymmetry of breakout trading into an actual edge. The
strategy loses small and often; it needs the winners to be large. A trailing
exit lets a winner run for as long as the move lasts, then gives back only the
last portion — while the stop, which never moves further away, caps the
downside at the planned 1%.

It also removes the two hardest discretionary decisions in trading: when to
take profit, and whether "it's just a pullback". Both get answered by a rule
written before there was any money on the line.

## When it fails

**It always gives back the top.** By construction you exit after the move has
turned, never at the high. If that bothers you, you will be tempted to add
profit targets, and profit targets cap the large winners the whole strategy
depends on. Resist.

**It exits on ordinary pullbacks in a still-intact trend.** A sharp two-day
dip through the average ends a trade that would have continued for months. The
shorter the exit window, the more often this happens.

**In choppy markets it compounds the entry problem.** The same conditions that
produce false breakouts produce premature exits, so a range-bound period costs
you on both ends.

## In gold

Gold's advances often pause for weeks rather than correcting, drifting sideways
while the 20-day average catches up from below. A 20-day exit tolerates that
reasonably well — a flat market pulls the average toward price rather than
through it.

Gold's *declines* are a different matter and this is where the exit earns its
keep. When gold breaks, it tends to break hard and keep going, because the same
macro forces that drove it up unwind. Getting out on the first close below the
short average, rather than waiting for the stop, frequently saves a large
fraction of the position value.

Watch the interaction with the overnight gap, though. If gold falls sharply
overnight, the close below the average and the stop being breached can happen
on the same bar — and the fill is the open, wherever that was. The exit rule
cannot outrun a gap any more than the stop can.
